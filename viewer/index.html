About MapHub

MapHub ranks each day's new arXiv papers in astrophysics (astro-ph) and artificial intelligence (cs.AI) for a community of researchers and groups, and shows who among them could answer questions about each paper.

  • Rows are the papers announced that day; columns are participants, shown by pseudonym.
  • Each cell is a relevance score from 0 to 100. A language model (Claude) assigns it by reading the paper's title, authors and abstract against the participant's research portfolio: 100 = they would have written it, 80 = coauthored it, 60 = would cite it, 30 = may broaden their view, 0 = outside their interests.
  • Select a participant (column name) to see the papers most relevant to them, and the participants with the most similar interests. Select a paper (its title) to see who it matters to most: likely the best people to ask about it. Similarity, r, is the correlation of that day's scores.
  • Hover over a cell for its score, or over an arXiv number for the paper's link. The arrows and the date box move between days; weekends and holidays have no listing.

Participants are researchers and research groups who have each provided a short research portfolio describing their work; the scores come from these portfolios. Columns carry pseudonyms rather than names. If you would like to join, please email kailicao@umich.edu.

MapHub is a carpentry of the Eric and Wendy Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellowship at the Michigan Institute for Data and AI in Society (MIDAS), led by Kaili Cao with teammates Kyle Heyblom and Eva Albalghiti. Its code and daily tables are public on GitHub; titles and authors come from DataCite, which holds arXiv's DOI records.

MapHub is under development. Its scores come from a language model and can be wrong: treat them as pointers to papers and people, not as judgments of anyone's work. Questions and suggestions are welcome: kailicao@umich.edu.
